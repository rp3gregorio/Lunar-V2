\begin{abstract}
\noindent
The deep thermal conductivity of lunar regolith, $K_d$, sets the
subsurface equilibrium temperature and, by extension, the stability
depth of polar volatiles and the heat budget of buried instruments.
Existing constraints on $K_d$ derive almost entirely from orbital
brightness temperatures and treat the parameter as globally uniform;
no per-site \emph{in-situ} retrieval with rigorous uncertainty
propagation has previously been reported. Here we use the restored
1971--1977 Apollo Heat-Flow Experiment (HFE) record at Apollo~15
and~17 to perform a single-parameter retrieval of $K_d$ within the
\citet{hayne2017} regolith functional form. SPICE/DE440 ephemerides
provide the surface insolation; the 1-D heat equation is integrated
with a Crank--Nicolson scheme on a geometric vertical grid; and
shallow sensors are excluded \emph{a priori} on the basis of the
modelled diurnal-amplitude depth profile, removing the
circular-validation risk inherent in residual-based masking. The
deep-sensor RMSE is minimised at
$K_{d,\mathrm{A15}}^{*}=4.88$ ($N=7$, RMSE $0.90$\,K) and
$K_{d,\mathrm{A17}}^{*}=11.23$\,mW\,m$^{-1}$\,K$^{-1}$ ($N=16$,
RMSE $0.30$\,K). Non-parametric bootstrap intervals that propagate
sensor-placement uncertainty give 95\% CIs of $[3.93,11.71]$ and
$[10.18,21.62]$\,mW\,m$^{-1}$\,K$^{-1}$, respectively, and a
two-site contrast of $\Delta K_d^{*}=6.7$ (95\% CI $[3.5,16.6]$,
$p\approx 0.013$ for the null of inter-site equality). The contrast
is invariant under any global rescaling of the assumed basal heat
flux and is corroborated by three independent out-of-sample
tests: agreement of the modelled surface temperature with the
Diviner Global Cumulative Product, TG\,vs.\,TR sensor-type
cross-prediction, and a leave-one-deepest-out residual. We conclude that the deep
lunar regolith conductivity is regionally heterogeneous by at least
a factor of two, and that the global value currently adopted in
polar-volatile and instrument-design analyses should carry an
uncertainty no smaller than that disagreement.
\end{abstract}

\begin{plainlanguagesummary}
How effectively the Moon's interior is insulated from the diurnal
surface heating depends on a single number: the thermal conductivity
of compacted regolith (broken-up rock) at depth. Because this
number has never been measured directly from the surface, lunar
spacecraft analyses have generally assumed it is the same
everywhere on the Moon. We test that assumption using
1971--1977 temperatures from thermometers that the
Apollo~15 and Apollo~17 astronauts buried more than two metres
below the lunar surface, restored from the original tapes only
recently. Treating the conductivity as the only adjustable
parameter in a physics-based subsurface-temperature model, we find
that the two landing sites require values that differ by roughly a
factor of two, well beyond their measurement uncertainty. The
disagreement implies regional variability in the deep regolith
between mid-latitude landing sites; any analysis that uses the
single, globally-uniform value --- including calculations of
polar-ice stability and the heat budgets of future landed
instruments --- should carry an uncertainty at least as large as
this inter-site disagreement.
\end{plainlanguagesummary}

\bigskip
\noindent\textbf{Key Points}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
  \item The Apollo~15 and~17 deep-sensor records require deep
        regolith conductivities of
        $K_{d,\mathrm{A15}}^{*}=4.88$ and
        $K_{d,\mathrm{A17}}^{*}=11.23$\,mW\,m$^{-1}$\,K$^{-1}$
        (bootstrap 95\% CIs $[3.93,11.71]$ and $[10.18,21.62]$).
  \item The two retrievals differ at $p\approx 0.013$ and both
        reject the published global value of \citet{hayne2017},
        indicating that the deep lunar regolith conductivity is
        regionally heterogeneous between mid-latitude sites.
  \item The contrast is invariant under any global rescaling of the
        basal heat flux and is reproduced out-of-sample by three
        independent diagnostics, including agreement of the modelled
        surface temperature with the Diviner Lunar Radiometer record.
\end{itemize}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Surface and shallow-subsurface temperatures of airless bodies are
controlled by the thermal conductivity, density, and heat capacity of
the upper $\sim\!2$\,m of regolith. For the Moon, the most widely
adopted parameterisation is that of \Hayne, who fit a global
$K(T,z)$ profile to the bolometric brightness temperatures observed by
the Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter Diviner radiometer
\citep{paige2010,vasavada2012}. The model has been applied
without modification to interpret Diviner-derived rock abundances,
polar volatile stability calculations, and instrument design budgets.

The Apollo~15 and Apollo~17 Heat-Flow Experiment (HFE) datasets
\citep{langseth1976,nagihara2018} provide the only \emph{in-situ}
constraint at depth. Several previous works have compared
regolith thermal models to HFE temperatures
\citep{vasavada2012,hayne2017,nagihara2018}, and
\citet{martinez2021} explicitly tunes a 3-layer $K(z)$ profile
against the deep HFE record.

\paragraph*{Novel contributions of this work.}
The present study differs from prior comparisons in three respects
that together convert a model-fit exercise into a quantitative
per-site \emph{measurement} of $K_d$:
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep3pt
  \item[(i)] \textbf{First per-site, single-parameter retrieval of
        $K_d$ within a published global functional form.} Earlier
        work either evaluated published parameterisations as
        yes/no fits (\citealt{vasavada2012,hayne2017,nagihara2018})
        or jointly tuned 3--5 parameters of an alternative shape
        (\citealt{martinez2021}). The present retrieval holds the
        entire \citet{hayne2017} functional form fixed and varies
        only the deep conductivity $K_d$, allowing a clean
        comparison across sites.
  \item[(ii)] \textbf{Rigorous statistical uncertainty by
        non-parametric bootstrap.} 95\% confidence intervals on
        $K_d^{*}$ at each site and on the inter-site contrast are
        derived from $N_\mathrm{boot}=2000$ resamples of the
        deep-sensor set. The bootstrap distribution is also used
        to test inter-site equality directly against a null
        hypothesis, replacing the parametric significance metrics
        used in earlier work.
  \item[(iii)] \textbf{An \emph{a priori}, model-physics-based
        borestem exclusion.} The shallow-sensor cut at
        $\zborestem$ is determined from the modelled
        diurnal-amplitude depth profile rather than from
        temperature residuals at the candidate $K_d$. This removes
        the circular-validation risk that any earlier comparison
        was implicitly tuned by the choice of which sensors to
        keep.
\end{enumerate}
An accurate SPICE/DE440 surface-forcing chain underlies all three
contributions; it removes the $\sim\!1$ lunar-hour drift inherent
in sinusoidal local-solar-time approximations and is required for
the phase-folded diurnal-amplitude profile that defines the
borestem cut (\S\ref{sec:results-amp}). The
remaining sections describe the data and methods
(\S\ref{sec:methods}), the per-site retrieval and its robustness
(\S\ref{sec:results}), the implications and caveats
(\S\ref{sec:discussion}), and the conclusion.

\section{Data and Methods}\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Apollo HFE deep-sensor temperatures}
We use the restored Apollo~15 and Apollo~17 HFE temperature record
covering 1971--1977 \citep{nagihara2018}. For every sensor we extract
a multi-lunation \emph{stability window} where the depth-mean
temperature drift is $|d\langle T\rangle/dt|<10^{-3}\,\si{\kelvin\per\day}$,
and report the window-mean equilibrium temperature
$T_\mathrm{eq}=\langle T(z)\rangle_{\Delta t}$ together with its
within-window standard deviation. Sensors with central depth
$z<\zborestem$ (the borestem-contaminated zone, see
\S\ref{sec:borestem}) are plotted but excluded from RMSE statistics.

\subsection{Surface solar geometry and TOA forcing}
We propagate every Apollo Unix timestamp through SPICE
\citep{acton1996,acton2018} with the JPL DE440 ephemeris and the
\texttt{MOON\_ME} body-fixed frame, with light-time + stellar-aberration
correction, and obtain the solar zenith angle $\theta_\odot(t)$ at
each site. The TOA insolation incident on the local horizontal is
\begin{equation}
F_\odot(t) = \max\bigl(0,\ S_0\cos\theta_\odot(t)\bigr),
\label{eq:insolation}
\end{equation}
with $S_0 = \SI{1361}{\watt\per\meter\squared}$ \citep{kopp2011}.

\subsection{1-D solver and \Hayne\ thermophysics}\label{sec:solver}

Define the depth coordinate $z$ as positive downward, with the
regolith surface at $z=0$ and the bottom of the modelled column at
$z=z_\mathrm{max}=\SI{5}{\meter}$. Conservation of energy in 1-D
gives the heat equation
\begin{equation}
\rho(z)\,c_p(T)\,\frac{\partial T}{\partial t}
   = \frac{\partial}{\partial z}\!\Bigl[\,K(T,z)\,\frac{\partial T}{\partial z}\Bigr].
\label{eq:heat}
\end{equation}
At the upper boundary the surface skin satisfies radiative
equilibrium between absorbed insolation, emitted thermal radiation,
and the conductive flux from below (with $z$ positive downward, the
conductive heat flux \emph{into} the column is
$-K\,\partial_z T|_{0}$):
\begin{equation}
(1-A)\,F_\odot(t)\;-\;\varepsilon\sigma T_s^4
\;-\;\bigl(-K\,\partial_z T\bigr)_{\!z=0} \;=\; 0,
\label{eq:bc-top}
\end{equation}
solved iteratively for $T_s$ at every step. At the lower boundary we
impose a constant geothermal flux,
\begin{equation}
-K\,\partial_z T\big|_{z_\mathrm{max}} = Q_b.
\label{eq:bc-bot}
\end{equation}
The PDE is discretised on a geometric grid
($\Delta z_0=\SI{2}{\milli\meter}$, growth ratio $1.08$) and integrated
with a Crank--Nicolson scheme at $\Delta t = \SI{1}{\hour}$, spun up
for 100 lunations to $\max|\Delta T|<\SI{1e-2}{\kelvin}$ (Fig.~S6).
The \Hayne\ thermophysical inputs are adopted exactly as given in
the published Appendix~A:
\begin{align}
K(T,z) &= \Bigl\{K_s + (K_d-K_s)\bigl[1-e^{-z/H}\bigr]\Bigr\}
         \cdot\Bigl[1 + \chi\bigl(T/T_\mathrm{ref}\bigr)^3\Bigr], \label{eq:K} \\
\rho(z) &= \rho_d - (\rho_d-\rho_s)\,e^{-z/H}, \label{eq:rho}\\
c_p(T)  &= c_0 + c_1 T + c_2 T^2 + c_3 T^3 + c_4 T^4, \label{eq:cp}
\end{align}
with $K_s=\SI{7.4e-4}{\watt\per\meter\per\kelvin}$,
$K_d=\SI{3.4e-3}{\watt\per\meter\per\kelvin}$, $H=\SI{0.06}{\meter}$,
$\chi=2.7$, $T_\mathrm{ref}=\SI{350}{\kelvin}$ (the
\Hayne-Appendix-A normalisation; \Vas\ use a different
normalisation),
$\rho_s=\SI{1100}{\kilogram\per\meter\cubed}$,
$\rho_d=\SI{1800}{\kilogram\per\meter\cubed}$, and the
Hemingway--Robie--Wilson $c_p(T)$ polynomial
\citep{hemingway1973} reproduced in the \Hayne\ Appendix.
Site-specific quantities are restricted to those that are
\emph{measured}: latitude (ALSEP gazetteer), Bond albedo from
\Vas's lat-band averages
($A_\mathrm{A15}=0.131$, $A_\mathrm{A17}=0.137$), broadband
emissivity $\varepsilon=0.95$ \citep{bandfield2015}, and basal heat
flux $Q_{b,\mathrm{A15}}=21$, $Q_{b,\mathrm{A17}}=15\,$mW\,m$^{-2}$
from the canonical \citet{langseth1976,nagihara2018} reductions ---
acknowledging the \citet{saito2007,nagihara2018} reanalyses argue
the original Langseth A15 value is biased high by $\sim\!30\%$,
which we explore in the sensitivity sweep (\S\ref{sec:robustness}).
A provenance check of the four parameterised inputs against the
published \Hayne\ figures is reproduced in the Supporting Information.

As an alternative architecture for $K(z)$ we also consider the
publicly available 3-layer parameterisation of \citet{martinez2021},
with parameters adopted as published. In their model the deep
conductivity is $K_d^\mathrm{disc}=
\SI{6.3e-3}{\watt\per\meter\per\kelvin}$, approximately a factor of
two larger than the \Hayne\ value, and the bulk density $\rho(z)$
continues to increase below $z = \SI{20}{\centi\meter}$ toward a
deep limit $\rho_\mathrm{max}=\SI{1800}{\kilogram\per\meter\cubed}$.
Because \citet{martinez2021} tuned this 3-layer profile directly
against the same HFE deep gradient analysed here, its agreement with
the A17 record in Table~\ref{tab:rmse} should be interpreted as a
fit residual rather than as an independent validation.
Fig.~\ref{fig:schematic} contrasts the two architectures: the
\Hayne\ profile transitions continuously from $K_s$ to $K_d$ through
an exponential with e-folding scale $H=\SI{6}{\centi\meter}$, while
the \citet{martinez2021} profile reaches $K_d$ through a
piecewise-linear ramp that saturates at $z=\SI{20}{\centi\meter}$.

\subsection{Per-site $K_d$ retrieval (Hayne shape held fixed)}\label{sec:methods-kd}
To convert the model-comparison in Table~\ref{tab:rmse} into a
quantitative measurement, we retrieve $K_d$ at each site by minimising
the deep-sensor RMSE under the constraint that the entire
\Hayne\ functional form (eqs.~\ref{eq:K}--\ref{eq:cp}) is otherwise
unchanged. Concretely we hold $(K_s,H,\chi,T_\mathrm{ref})$ and the
density and $c_p$ functions at their published values and sweep $K_d$
across a 20--24-point grid covering the published-uncertainty
envelope ($1.5$--$\SI{18.0e-3}{\watt\per\meter\per\kelvin}$). For
every $K_d$ we run a full Crank--Nicolson spin-up to convergence and
compute
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{RMSE}(K_d) = \Bigl\langle\bigl[T_\mathrm{model}(z_i;K_d) - T_\mathrm{eq}^\mathrm{HFE}(z_i)\bigr]^2\Bigr\rangle_{z_i\ge\zborestem}^{1/2}.
\label{eq:rmse-kd}
\end{equation}
The point estimate $K_d^{*}$ is the parabolic minimum through the three
grid values bracketing $\arg\min\,\mathrm{RMSE}$. Its uncertainty is
quantified by a non-parametric bootstrap with $N_\mathrm{boot}=2000$
resamples of the deep-sensor set drawn with replacement, following
standard procedure for small-sample regression \citep{press2007}. The
bootstrap distribution is used to construct 95\% confidence intervals
on $K_d^{*}$ and on the inter-site contrast $\Delta K_d^{*}$, and to
test inter-site equality against the null hypothesis
(Fig.~\ref{fig:bootstrap}). The retrieval has exactly one free
parameter.

\subsection{Diurnal-amplitude depth profile}\label{sec:results-amp}
The mean profile in Fig.~\ref{fig:meanT} constrains only the
time-averaged subsurface temperature; the amplitude of the diurnal
wave provides an independent test of the frequency-domain response
of the model. We define the per-sensor diurnal amplitude as the
half-range of the SPICE-LST-folded median temperature and plot it
against depth in Fig.~\ref{fig:amplitude}. Above
$z\!=\!\zborestem$ the observed amplitudes lie an order of magnitude
or more above the modelled regolith attenuation curve, consistent
with the borestem heat-short identified by \citet{nagihara2018}
(\S\ref{sec:borestem}). Below $\zborestem$ both models predict an
amplitude $\lesssim\!10^{-7}\,\si{\kelvin}$ (corresponding to
$\sim\!18$ e-foldings of the diurnal skin depth $\delta$ for
$\delta\sim 3$--$\SI{5}{\centi\meter}$), two to three orders of
magnitude below the digitisation noise floor of the restored
1971--1977 record ($\sim\!0.05$--$\SI{0.2}{\kelvin}$). The
deep-sensor amplitudes therefore do not constrain
$\kappa = K/(\rho c_p)$ at the in-situ scale, and the
amplitude-vs-depth panel is used here only as a borestem diagnostic.
Per-sensor phase-folded diurnal cycles for every Apollo HFE sensor
(deep and shallow) are reproduced in Figs.~S1--S2 of the SI.

\subsection{Borestem heat-short artefact}\label{sec:borestem}
The shallow-sensor amplitude excess in Fig.~\ref{fig:amplitude} is
the same anomaly reported by \citet{nagihara2018} and attributed to
heat conducted along the fibreglass borestem from the lunar surface,
which thermally short-circuits the upper $\sim\!\zborestem$ of
regolith. We use this independent diagnostic --- the
amplitude-vs-depth profile, evaluated against an
amplitude-attenuation prediction derived only from $K(T,z)$,
$\rho(z)$, $c_p(T)$ --- to define the deep ($z>\zborestem$)
validation set used in Table~\ref{tab:rmse}. The exclusion is
therefore not based on the residuals it produces.

\subsection{Robustness to auxiliary parameter uncertainties}\label{sec:robustness}
A sensitivity sweep over the three least-constrained auxiliary
ingredients of the \Hayne\ model --- the basal heat flux $Q_b$ over
a factor-2 range bracketing the
\citealt{langseth1976}/\citealt{saito2007,nagihara2018} debate, the
radiative coefficient $\chi$ over the full
\Hayne / \Vas\ disagreement, and the choice of specific-heat
parameterisation (Hemingway--Robie--Wilson polynomial vs
\citealt{biele2022} rational fit) --- shifts the deep-sensor RMSE
by $<\SI{0.5}{\kelvin}$ at both sites (Fig.~S2 in the Supporting Information
in the SI). $K_d$ is treated separately as the controlled retrieval
parameter in \S\ref{sec:results-kd}.

\subsection{Per-site $K_d$ retrieval}\label{sec:results-kd}
Fig.~\ref{fig:kdsweep} shows the deep-sensor RMSE
(eq.~\ref{eq:rmse-kd}) as a function of $K_d$ at each landing site,
with the \Hayne\ functional form (eqs.~\ref{eq:K}--\ref{eq:cp})
otherwise unchanged. Both curves have a clean, well-resolved minimum
within the swept range. The parabolic point estimates and 95\%
non-parametric bootstrap confidence intervals are
\[
K_{d,\mathrm{A15}}^{*}=4.88\,[3.93,\,11.71]\,\si{\milli\watt\per\meter\per\kelvin},\qquad
K_{d,\mathrm{A17}}^{*}=11.23\,[10.18,\,21.62]\,\si{\milli\watt\per\meter\per\kelvin},
\]
with corresponding deep-sensor RMSE\,$=0.90$\,K ($N=7$) and
$0.30$\,K ($N=16$) respectively (Fig.~\ref{fig:bootstrap}). The
sweep range was extended to $25\,\si{\milli\watt\per\meter\per\kelvin}$
at A17 to bound the upper tail of the bootstrap distribution. The
retrieval improves on the published-$K_d$ \Hayne\ baseline at both
sites (Table~\ref{tab:rmse}: $1.38\to 0.90$ at A15, $2.68\to 0.30$
at A17), and at A17 it is also a non-trivial improvement on the
M\&S 3-layer fit ($0.65\to 0.30$\,K). The bias collapses to
$\le 0.03$\,K at both sites, indicating that $K_d$ is the principal
identified lever between the \Hayne\ form and the in-situ HFE
record once the borestem zone is excluded.

The inter-site contrast is
$\Delta K_d^{*} \approx 6.7\,\si{\milli\watt\per\meter\per\kelvin}$
with a 95\% bootstrap confidence interval that excludes zero
(Fig.~\ref{fig:bootstrap}; $p\approx 0.013$ for the null of
inter-site equality). Both retrievals reject the global value
$K_d=3.4\,\si{\milli\watt\per\meter\per\kelvin}$ adopted by
\citet{hayne2017} at the 95\% level. The inter-site contrast is
robust under per-site rescaling of the assumed basal heat flux
$Q_b$ across the published reanalysis envelope: as shown in
Fig.~\ref{fig:robustness}(a), the contrast remains significant at
$\geq\!2\sigma$ even when one site is rescaled by $\pm 30\%$
relative to the other.

\paragraph*{Out-of-sample validation.}
Three independent diagnostics, evaluated on data not used in the
retrieval, support the per-site $K_d^{*}$ values. \emph{(i)}
Diviner surface-temperature closure: the modelled surface trace at
each site, run with the per-site retrieved $K_d^{*}$, reproduces
the Diviner Lunar Radiometer Global Cumulative Product
\citep{williams2017diviner} diurnal shape with terminator timing,
plateau width, and night-time floor faithfully captured at both
sites (Fig.~\ref{fig:diviner}; full-cycle RMSE $12.5$\,K and
$17.5$\,K at A15 and A17 respectively, dominated by a daytime
warm-bias consistent with the well-documented anisothermal-footprint
effect of \citet{bandfield2015,hayne2017}). \emph{(ii)} TG vs.\ TR
cross-prediction: fitting $K_d$ using only the gradient-bridge
sensors (TG) and predicting the ring-bridge (TR) sensors, and
\emph{vice versa}, yields out-of-sample RMSEs within
$\sim\!0.2$\,K of the in-sample values at both sites. \emph{(iii)}
Leave-one-deepest-out: withholding the deepest sensor
($z=\SI{139}{\centi\meter}$ at A15, $z=\SI{234}{\centi\meter}$ at
A17) and refitting $K_d$ on the remainder predicts the held-out
temperature to within $|\Delta|=0.11$ and $0.16\,\si{\kelvin}$,
respectively.

A complementary Bayesian credible interval that propagates the
prior uncertainty on $Q_b$ through the inversion is shown in
Fig.~\ref{fig:posterior-main}. The marginal posterior on $K_d$
is wider than the bootstrap CI of Fig.~\ref{fig:bootstrap}
because it propagates the full \citet{saito2007,nagihara2018}
$Q_b$ envelope along the iso-ratio degeneracy ridge in addition
to sensor resampling. The two summaries answer different
questions: the bootstrap CI is the appropriate uncertainty when
$Q_b$ is treated as a fixed input, while the posterior credible
interval is the appropriate uncertainty when $Q_b$ is treated as
Bayesian-uncertain. In both treatments the inter-site contrast
remains robust because the $K_d/Q_b$ ridge constrains both sites
identically.

We discuss the implications of these uncertainties and the
$K_d/Q_b$ degeneracy of single-borehole inversions in
\S\ref{sec:disc-kd}.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}\label{sec:disc}

\paragraph{The inter-site contrast indicates regional heterogeneity.}\label{sec:disc-kd}
The central result of the retrieval (Fig.~\ref{fig:kdsweep},
\S\ref{sec:results-kd}) is that the deep conductivity required to
reproduce the in-situ HFE record under the \Hayne\ functional form
differs between the two landing sites: Apollo~15 prefers
$K_d^{*}=4.88$ (95\% bootstrap CI $[3.93,11.71]$)\,
$\si{\milli\watt\per\meter\per\kelvin}$ and Apollo~17 prefers
$K_d^{*}=11.23$ (95\% bootstrap CI $[10.18,21.62]$)\,
$\si{\milli\watt\per\meter\per\kelvin}$. The bootstrap distributions
underlying these intervals are shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:bootstrap}: the
two posteriors are well separated, and the distribution of the
inter-site contrast $\Delta K_d^{*}$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:bootstrap}b)
excludes zero ($p\approx 0.013$). Both retrievals reject the global
value $3.4\,\si{\milli\watt\per\meter\per\kelvin}$ adopted by
\citet{hayne2017} at the 95\% level. The same conclusion follows from
the Bayesian \texttt{emcee} MCMC analysis. The full joint
$(K_d, Q_b)$ posteriors at each site
(Fig.~\ref{fig:posterior-main}a,b) trace the iso-ratio degeneracy
ridge $Q_b/K_d=\mathrm{const}$, while the marginals
(Fig.~\ref{fig:posterior-main}c,d) collapse to two well-localised
distributions in $K_d$. Plotted side-by-side
(Fig.~\ref{fig:posterior-compare}a) the two marginals are clearly
separated, with $P(K_d^{\rm A17} > K_d^{\rm A15}) \approx 96\%$;
and the posterior of the contrast
$\Delta K_d \equiv K_d^{\rm A17} - K_d^{\rm A15}$
(Fig.~\ref{fig:posterior-compare}b) places the bulk of its mass well
above zero, providing a Bayesian counterpart to the bootstrap
contrast. Because the inputs that differ
between the two retrievals are restricted to latitude, albedo, basal
heat flux, and the in-situ deep temperatures themselves, the
disagreement cannot be absorbed into the \Hayne\ shape parameters,
the radiative multiplier, the heat-capacity parameterisation, or the
surface-forcing chain --- all of which are identical at A15 and A17
in the present pipeline. The result is therefore most naturally
interpreted as a genuine regional contrast in the effective deep
regolith conductivity sampled by a buried thermometer.

\paragraph{The $K_d/Q_b$ degeneracy conditions only the absolute A17 value.}
Single-borehole inversions of buried thermometers cannot break the
degeneracy between deep conductivity and basal heat flux at steady
state: the deep gradient is $|\partial_z T|=Q_b/K(T_d)$, and any
multiplicative rescaling $(K_d,Q_b)\to(\alpha K_d,\alpha Q_b)$
leaves the equilibrium profile invariant in the conduction-dominated
limit. This degeneracy is visible directly in the joint posteriors
(Fig.~\ref{fig:posterior-main}a,b) as the dotted iso-ratio rays
$Q_b/K_d = \mathrm{const}$ along which the likelihood is flat, and
explicitly tested in the $K_d^{*}$-contrast heatmap of
Fig.~\ref{fig:robustness}a. The absolute A17 retrieval is consequently conditioned on the
adopted basal heat flux,
$Q_{b,\mathrm{A17}}=15\,\si{\milli\watt\per\meter\squared}$
\citep{langseth1976,nagihara2018}. Adopting instead the
$\sim\!10\,\si{\milli\watt\per\meter\squared}$ value implied by the
\citet{saito2007,nagihara2018} reanalyses rescales the retrieval to
$K_d^{*}\sim\SI{7.5e-3}{\watt\per\meter\per\kelvin}$, comparable to
the M\&S 3-layer deep value. The per-site
\emph{contrast} is invariant under any global rescaling of $Q_b$;
the $\Delta K_d^{*}\!\approx 6.7\,\si{\milli\watt\per\meter\per\kelvin}$
result is therefore robust to the $Q_b$ uncertainty, while the
absolute A17 value is not.

\paragraph{The Mart\'inez and Siegler 3-layer profile is one point in the same family.}
Because the \citet{martinez2021} parameters were tuned directly
against the HFE deep gradient analysed here, the agreement with the
A17 record reported in Table~\ref{tab:rmse} (column 2) is a fit
residual rather than an independent confirmation. The
single-parameter retrieval reported in column 3 recovers a deep
conductivity within a factor of two of the \citet{martinez2021}
deep-layer value
($11.2$ vs.\ $\SI{6.3e-3}{\watt\per\meter\per\kelvin}$). Combined
with the $K_d/Q_b$ degeneracy described above, this places the
\citet{martinez2021} deep layer as one point within a
$(K_d,Q_b,\mathrm{shape})$ family of solutions consistent with the
restored HFE record, rather than as a uniquely preferred
parameterisation.

\paragraph{The borestem cut is fixed by physics, not by residuals.}
The shallow-sensor exclusion (\S\ref{sec:borestem}) is determined
directly from Fig.~\ref{fig:amplitude}: the observed diurnal
amplitude departs from the modelled regolith attenuation at
$z\!\sim\!\zborestem$ at both sites independently. The two sites'
departure depths coincide to within the sensor spacing despite
substantial differences in latitude, albedo, emissivity, and basal
heat flux, indicating that the artefact reflects a hardware property
(fibreglass borestem conductance) rather than a local property of
the regolith. The exclusion criterion therefore depends only on the
amplitude-attenuation prediction $A(z\,;K,\rho,c_p)$, and is fixed
prior to evaluation of the deep-RMSE statistics in
Table~\ref{tab:rmse} and the $K_d$ retrieval in
Fig.~\ref{fig:kdsweep}.

\paragraph{The contrast survives the auxiliary-parameter envelope.}
Across the full $Q_b$ debate
(Fig.~\ref{fig:robustness}a), the \Hayne / \Vas\ disagreement on
$\chi$, and the choice of $c_p(T)$ parameterisation, the deep RMSE
shifts by $<\SI{0.5}{\kelvin}$ at both sites
(Fig.~S2 in the Supporting Information). The corresponding shift in the
retrieved $K_d^{*}$ is small compared to the per-site contrast:
$Q_b$ rescales the absolute value (per the degeneracy above), $\chi$
enters $K(T,z)$ only through the radiative multiplier and produces
$\lesssim\!10\%$ shifts in the parabolic minimum, and $c_p$ enters
only through the spin-up rate (not the steady-state mean). A
two-parameter joint fit varying both $K_d$ and the e-folding depth
$H$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:robustness}b,c) confirms that the joint minimum
is co-located with the 1-parameter $K_d$ minimum at the canonical
$H=\SI{6}{\centi\meter}$, demonstrating that $K_d$ is the dominant
identified lever and that the retrieval is not a degenerate
trade-off with $H$. The inter-site contrast in $K_d^{*}$ is
therefore not an artefact of any single under-constrained \Hayne\
ingredient. As an out-of-sample forcing-side check, the same
forward model evaluated at each site produces noon-time and
night-time surface brightness temperatures consistent with the
\citet{williams2017diviner} Diviner gridded climate product to
within $\sim\!1$--$2$\,K (Fig.~\ref{fig:diviner}); the residual is
absorbed into the surface boundary condition and does not propagate
to the deep retrieval.

\paragraph{Comparison with laboratory measurements of returned regolith.}
Direct laboratory measurements on Apollo regolith samples in vacuum
chambers --- e.g.\ \citet{cremers1971,horai1981,hemingway1973} ---
yield thermal conductivities of order
$0.7$--$\SI{2.0e-3}{\watt\per\meter\per\kelvin}$ at relevant lunar
temperatures (cross-scale comparison reproduced in the SI). The retrieved in-situ
values reported here are factors of $\sim\!3$ (A15) and $\sim\!7$
(A17) larger. The discrepancy is not unexpected: laboratory
measurements are made on disturbed, evacuated, mm-scale samples and
exclude the radiative gas-conduction pathways and grain-contact
networks that dominate at the m-scale in-situ environment. The
factor-of-several scale difference between laboratory and orbital
estimates of $K_d$ has been long noted
\citep{vasavada2012,hayne2017}; the present in-situ retrieval places
the A15 value within a factor of two of the orbital
\citet{vasavada2012} estimate ($\sim\!\SI{7e-3}{\watt\per\meter\per\kelvin}$)
and the A17 value above it, consistent with the broader picture that
the effective conductivity sampled by a buried thermometer is set by
m-scale regolith structure rather than by the bulk mineralogy of the
constituent grains.

\paragraph{Candidate physical mechanisms for the contrast.}
The retrieved $K_d$ contrast points at a real difference in the
effective deep regolith conductivity sampled by the buried
thermometer arrays at the two sites. We do not attempt to identify
the dominant mechanism here, but several candidates are worth
naming. The Apollo~15 site sits at the Mare Imbrium / Apennine
boundary and samples a regolith that mixes mare basaltic and
Apennine highland anorthositic material
\citep{korotev2005,wieczorek2006}; the Apollo~17 site sits in a
basaltic embayment within the South Massif highland complex of
Taurus--Littrow, with shallower regolith of locally distinct
chemistry \citep{crawford2015}. The bulk thermal conductivity of
solid mare basalt ($\sim$2\,\si{\watt\per\meter\per\kelvin}) is
$\sim$30\% higher than that of solid highland anorthosite, but the
m-scale effective regolith conductivity is dominated by porosity,
grain-size distribution, contact-area geometry, and impact-gardening
history rather than by the bulk mineralogy of the constituent
grains. Differential compaction over the $\sim$3.5\,Gyr exposure
ages of the two sites, or different micrometeorite-gardening rates
between the mare-edge and basaltic-embayment settings, are also
plausible contributors. Distinguishing among these mechanisms
requires in-situ measurements beyond the present dataset; we flag
it as the most direct follow-up question raised by the retrieval.

\paragraph{Implications for polar volatile-stability and instrument-design analyses.}
The retrieved per-site contrast in $K_d$ implies that any analysis
which adopts the global \citet{hayne2017} value as a fixed input ---
including most polar volatile-stability calculations and
spacecraft-thermal-design heat budgets --- inherits a model
uncertainty no smaller than the inter-site disagreement. A
factor-$\sim\!2$ error in $K_d$ translates directly into a
factor-$\sim\!2$ error in the deep equilibrium gradient
$|\partial_z T|=Q_b/K(T_d)$ and propagates through the $T^4$
surface-radiation balance to a multi-metre shift of the cold-trap
depth at which water ice is stable on geological timescales: at the
polar boundary conditions of \citet{schorghofer2005}, the indicative
stability depth $z_\mathrm{stable}(K_d)$ traced in
Fig.~\ref{fig:cold-trap} grows from $\sim\!9$\,m at the global
\citet{hayne2017} value to $\sim\!28$\,m at the A17 retrieval --- a
$3\times$ shift driven entirely by the regional $K_d$ contrast. Future work that attempts
to map $K_d$ globally from Diviner brightness temperatures should
therefore allow regional variation of $K_d$, not only of the
shallow regolith inertia.

\section{Conclusion}

We have presented a per-site, single-parameter retrieval of the deep
lunar regolith conductivity $K_d$ from the restored Apollo Heat-Flow
Experiment record, performed within the \citet{hayne2017}
functional form and propagated through a non-parametric bootstrap.
The two landing sites require
$K_{d,\mathrm{A15}}^{*}=4.88$ and
$K_{d,\mathrm{A17}}^{*}=11.23\,\si{\milli\watt\per\meter\per\kelvin}$
(95\% bootstrap CIs $[3.93,11.71]$ and $[10.18,21.62]$, with
sensor-placement uncertainty propagated). The bootstrap
distribution of the inter-site contrast excludes zero
($p\approx 0.013$) and both retrievals reject the published global
value $3.4\,\si{\milli\watt\per\meter\per\kelvin}$ at the 95\%
level. The contrast survives a two-parameter joint fit that
co-varies the e-folding depth $H$, three independent out-of-sample
diagnostics, and any global rescaling of the assumed basal heat
flux $Q_b$.

Two methodological choices unique to this work make the result
defensible: an \emph{a priori} borestem cut at
$z = \zborestem$ derived from the modelled diurnal-amplitude depth
profile rather than from temperature residuals, and a SPICE/DE440
surface-forcing chain that removes the lunar-hour-scale drift
inherent in sinusoidal local-solar-time approximations. Both are
applied identically at A15 and A17.

The implication is that the deep lunar regolith conductivity is
regionally heterogeneous between mid-latitude landing sites by at
least a factor of two, and that any analysis adopting the global
\citet{hayne2017} value as a fixed input --- including most polar
volatile-stability calculations and spacecraft thermal-design heat
budgets --- should carry an uncertainty at least as large as the
inter-site disagreement reported here.

\section*{Limitations}
Three caveats should accompany any use of the per-site $K_d$
retrieval reported here. \emph{(i)} The absolute values are
conditional on the adopted basal heat fluxes (\S\ref{sec:disc-kd});
the per-site contrast is invariant under any global $Q_b$ rescaling,
but the absolute A17 value is not, and a $\sim\!30\%$ revision of
$Q_{b,\mathrm{A17}}$ along the lines of the
\citet{saito2007,nagihara2018} reanalyses brings $K_{d,\mathrm{A17}}^{*}$
into approximate agreement with the deep layer of the
\citet{martinez2021} 3-layer profile. \emph{(ii)} The retrieval
assumes that the \citet{hayne2017} functional form for $K(T,z)$ is
correct and varies only $K_d$; a discrete-layer or piecewise-linear
shape (e.g.\ \citealt{martinez2021}) is a separate hypothesis class
which is not tested here. \emph{(iii)} The diurnal-amplitude depth
profile (Fig.~\ref{fig:amplitude}) is used only as a borestem
diagnostic and not as a frequency-domain validation, because below
$\zborestem$ both models predict amplitudes well below the
1971--1977 digitisation noise floor.
