\begin{abstract}
\noindent
We present a self-consistent single-pixel thermal pipeline for the
lunar regolith and use it to perform a quantitative, per-site
retrieval of the deep conductivity $K_d$ against the restored Apollo
Heat-Flow Experiment (HFE) record at Apollo~15 and~17. Surface
forcing is propagated through SPICE/DE440; the heat equation is
integrated with a Crank--Nicolson scheme; and shallow sensors are
excluded \emph{a priori} from the diurnal-amplitude depth profile,
not by residual masking. Holding the
\citet{hayne2017} regolith functional form fixed and varying only
$K_d$, the deep-sensor RMSE bottoms at
$K_{d,\mathrm{A15}}^{*} = 4.85\pm0.59$\,mW\,m$^{-1}$\,K$^{-1}$
(RMSE\,$=$\,0.90\,K, $N=7$) and at
$K_{d,\mathrm{A17}}^{*} = 11.23\pm0.61$\,mW\,m$^{-1}$\,K$^{-1}$
(RMSE\,$=$\,0.30\,K, $N=16$); the per-site contrast is
$6.4\pm0.9$\,mW\,m$^{-1}$\,K$^{-1}$ ($\sim\!7.5\sigma$ on a
leave-one-out jackknife). Both retrievals reject the global
\citet{hayne2017} value $K_d=3.4$\,mW\,m$^{-1}$\,K$^{-1}$ at
$\geq\!2\sigma$. The result argues for regional rather than
global $K_d$ in any application of the Hayne parameterisation to
in-situ thermometry; we discuss the residual $K_d/Q_b$ degeneracy
that makes the absolute A17 value model-dependent. Methodology,
including SPICE forcing, an a-priori borestem cut, and a parameter
sensitivity sweep over $Q_b$, $\chi$ and $c_p$, transfers without
modification to the Phase-2 LOLA polar pipeline.
\end{abstract}

\begin{plainlanguagesummary}
How well the Moon's surface insulates its interior depends on a
single number --- the thermal conductivity of compacted regolith at
depth. Because nobody has ever measured that number from the surface
alone, every spacecraft analysis has assumed one global value. We
use thermometers buried in the regolith by the Apollo~15 and~17
missions in 1971--1972, only recently restored, to retrieve this
number directly at each site. The two sites disagree at high
significance: A17 demands roughly twice the conductivity that A15
allows. The disagreement implies that the regolith varies regionally
even at non-polar mare/highland sites, and that the global value
adopted in lunar volatile-stability and instrument-design budgets
should carry an uncertainty equal to that disagreement.
\end{plainlanguagesummary}

\bigskip
\noindent\textbf{Key Points}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep1pt
  \item Per-site $K_d$ retrieval against Apollo HFE:
        $K_{d,\mathrm{A15}}^{*}=4.85\pm0.59$ and
        $K_{d,\mathrm{A17}}^{*}=11.23\pm0.61$\,mW\,m$^{-1}$\,K$^{-1}$.
  \item The two-site contrast ($\sim\!7.5\sigma$) rejects a single
        global $K_d$ for in-situ thermometry within the Hayne (2017)
        functional form.
  \item Methodology --- SPICE/DE440 forcing, a-priori borestem cut,
        end-to-end shadow closure --- is reusable verbatim by the
        Phase-2 LOLA polar pipeline.
\end{itemize}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Surface and shallow-subsurface temperatures of airless bodies are
controlled by the thermal conductivity, density, and heat capacity of
the upper $\sim\!2$\,m of regolith. For the Moon, the most widely
adopted parameterisation is that of \Hayne, who fit a global
$K(T,z)$ profile to the bolometric brightness temperatures observed by
the Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter Diviner radiometer
\citep{paige2010,vasavada2012}. The model has been applied
without modification to interpret Diviner-derived rock abundances,
polar volatile stability calculations, and instrument design budgets.

The Apollo~15 and Apollo~17 Heat-Flow Experiment (HFE) datasets
\citep{langseth1976,nagihara2018} provide the only \emph{in-situ}
constraint at depth. Several previous works compare regolith thermal
models to HFE temperatures \citep{vasavada2012,hayne2017,nagihara2018}
and \citet{martinez2021} explicitly tunes a 3-layer
$K(z)$ profile against the deep HFE record. Our contribution is not
``the first comparison'' but rather a small set of methodological
upgrades that we believe make subsequent comparisons cleaner:

\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
  \item a SPICE/DE440 surface-forcing chain that eliminates the
        $\sim\!1$\,lunar-hour drift inherent in sinusoidal-LST
        approximations and is essential for the phase-folded diurnal
        validation in \S\ref{sec:results-amp};
  \item an \emph{a priori} borestem exclusion criterion derived from
        the modelled diurnal-amplitude depth profile rather than from
        residuals, eliminating the circular-validation risk of
        residual-based masking; and
  \item an end-to-end shadow-coupled solver closure test
        (Fig.~S11) that constitutes a regression target for the
        Phase-2 LOLA polar pipeline.
\end{itemize}

The substantive scientific finding is the per-site retrieval of $K_d$
under the \Hayne\ functional form (\S\ref{sec:results-kd}), which
rejects a single global $K_d$ for in-situ application: A15 and A17
optima differ by $\sim\!7.5\sigma$ on a leave-one-out jackknife. We
discuss the unavoidable $K_d/Q_b$ degeneracy of single-borehole
inversions and the model-dependence of the absolute A17 value in
\S\ref{sec:disc}.

\section{Data and Methods}\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Apollo HFE deep-sensor temperatures}
We use the restored Apollo~15 and Apollo~17 HFE temperature record
covering 1971--1977 \citep{nagihara2018}. For every sensor we extract
a multi-lunation \emph{stability window} where the depth-mean
temperature drift is $|d\langle T\rangle/dt|<10^{-3}\,\si{\kelvin\per\day}$,
and report the window-mean equilibrium temperature
$T_\mathrm{eq}=\langle T(z)\rangle_{\Delta t}$ together with its
within-window standard deviation. Sensors with central depth
$z<\zborestem$ (the borestem-contaminated zone, see
\S\ref{sec:borestem}) are plotted but excluded from RMSE statistics.

\subsection{Surface solar geometry and TOA forcing}
We propagate every Apollo Unix timestamp through SPICE
\citep{acton1996,acton2018} with the JPL DE440 ephemeris and the
\texttt{MOON\_ME} body-fixed frame, with light-time + stellar-aberration
correction, and obtain the solar zenith angle $\theta_\odot(t)$ at
each site. The TOA insolation incident on the local horizontal is
\begin{equation}
F_\odot(t) = \max\bigl(0,\ S_0\cos\theta_\odot(t)\bigr),
\label{eq:insolation}
\end{equation}
with $S_0 = \SI{1361}{\watt\per\meter\squared}$ \citep{kopp2011}.

\subsection{1-D solver and \Hayne\ thermophysics}\label{sec:solver}

Define the depth coordinate $z$ as positive downward, with the
regolith surface at $z=0$ and the bottom of the modelled column at
$z=z_\mathrm{max}=\SI{5}{\meter}$. Conservation of energy in 1-D
gives the heat equation
\begin{equation}
\rho(z)\,c_p(T)\,\frac{\partial T}{\partial t}
   = \frac{\partial}{\partial z}\!\Bigl[\,K(T,z)\,\frac{\partial T}{\partial z}\Bigr].
\label{eq:heat}
\end{equation}
At the upper boundary the surface skin satisfies radiative
equilibrium between absorbed insolation, emitted thermal radiation,
and the conductive flux from below (with $z$ positive downward, the
conductive heat flux \emph{into} the column is
$-K\,\partial_z T|_{0}$):
\begin{equation}
(1-A)\,F_\odot(t)\;-\;\varepsilon\sigma T_s^4
\;-\;\bigl(-K\,\partial_z T\bigr)_{\!z=0} \;=\; 0,
\label{eq:bc-top}
\end{equation}
solved iteratively for $T_s$ at every step. At the lower boundary we
impose a constant geothermal flux,
\begin{equation}
-K\,\partial_z T\big|_{z_\mathrm{max}} = Q_b.
\label{eq:bc-bot}
\end{equation}
The PDE is discretised on a geometric grid
($\Delta z_0=\SI{2}{\milli\meter}$, growth ratio $1.08$) and integrated
with a Crank--Nicolson scheme at $\Delta t = \SI{1}{\hour}$, spun up
for 100 lunations to $\max|\Delta T|<\SI{1e-2}{\kelvin}$ (Fig.~S6).
The \Hayne\ thermophysical inputs are imported \emph{verbatim} from
the published Appendix~A:
\begin{align}
K(T,z) &= \Bigl\{K_s + (K_d-K_s)\bigl[1-e^{-z/H}\bigr]\Bigr\}
         \cdot\Bigl[1 + \chi\bigl(T/T_\mathrm{ref}\bigr)^3\Bigr], \label{eq:K} \\
\rho(z) &= \rho_d - (\rho_d-\rho_s)\,e^{-z/H}, \label{eq:rho}\\
c_p(T)  &= c_0 + c_1 T + c_2 T^2 + c_3 T^3 + c_4 T^4, \label{eq:cp}
\end{align}
with $K_s=\SI{7.4e-4}{\watt\per\meter\per\kelvin}$,
$K_d=\SI{3.4e-3}{\watt\per\meter\per\kelvin}$, $H=\SI{0.06}{\meter}$,
$\chi=2.7$, $T_\mathrm{ref}=\SI{350}{\kelvin}$ (the
\Hayne-Appendix-A normalisation; \Vas\ use a different
normalisation),
$\rho_s=\SI{1100}{\kilogram\per\meter\cubed}$,
$\rho_d=\SI{1800}{\kilogram\per\meter\cubed}$, and the
Hemingway--Robie--Wilson $c_p(T)$ polynomial
\citep{hemingway1973} reproduced in the \Hayne\ Appendix.
Site-specific quantities are restricted to those that are
\emph{measured}: latitude (ALSEP gazetteer), Bond albedo from
\Vas's lat-band averages
($A_\mathrm{A15}=0.131$, $A_\mathrm{A17}=0.137$), broadband
emissivity $\varepsilon=0.95$ \citep{bandfield2015}, and basal heat
flux $Q_{b,\mathrm{A15}}=21$, $Q_{b,\mathrm{A17}}=15\,$mW\,m$^{-2}$
from the canonical \citet{langseth1976,nagihara2018} reductions ---
acknowledging the \citet{saito2007,nagihara2018} reanalyses argue
the original Langseth A15 value is biased high by $\sim\!30\%$,
which we explore in the sensitivity sweep (\S\ref{sec:robustness}).
Fig.~\ref{fig:hayne-props} verifies that the four parameterised
inputs match the published \Hayne\ figures.

The \emph{discrete} alternative is the publicly available 3-layer
parameterisation of \citet{martinez2021}, with parameters as listed
in their Table; in particular $K_d^\mathrm{disc}=
\SI{6.3e-3}{\watt\per\meter\per\kelvin}$, almost a factor of two
larger than the \Hayne\ value, and $\rho(z)$ continues to grow below
$z = \SI{20}{\centi\meter}$ towards a deep limit
$\rho_\mathrm{max}=\SI{1800}{\kilogram\per\meter\cubed}$. We use
\citet{martinez2021}'s \emph{published} parameters; nothing is
re-fitted in this work. We note for the record that
\citet{martinez2021} explicitly tuned this 3-layer profile against the
HFE deep gradient, so its agreement with A17 in
Table~\ref{tab:rmse} should be read as a fit residual rather than
an independent validation.

\subsection{Per-site $K_d$ retrieval (Hayne shape held fixed)}\label{sec:methods-kd}
To convert the model-comparison in Table~\ref{tab:rmse} into a
quantitative measurement, we retrieve $K_d$ at each site by minimising
the deep-sensor RMSE under the constraint that the entire
\Hayne\ functional form (eqs.~\ref{eq:K}--\ref{eq:cp}) is otherwise
unchanged. Concretely we hold $(K_s,H,\chi,T_\mathrm{ref})$ and the
density and $c_p$ functions at their published values and sweep $K_d$
across a 20--24-point grid covering the published-uncertainty
envelope ($1.5$--$\SI{18.0e-3}{\watt\per\meter\per\kelvin}$). For
every $K_d$ we run a full Crank--Nicolson spin-up to convergence and
compute
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{RMSE}(K_d) = \Bigl\langle\bigl[T_\mathrm{model}(z_i;K_d) - T_\mathrm{eq}^\mathrm{HFE}(z_i)\bigr]^2\Bigr\rangle_{z_i\ge\zborestem}^{1/2}.
\label{eq:rmse-kd}
\end{equation}
The point estimate $K_d^{*}$ is the parabolic minimum through the three
grid values bracketing $\arg\min\,\mathrm{RMSE}$; its uncertainty
$\sigma_{K_d^{*}}$ is the leave-one-out jackknife standard error
\citep[][\S5.6]{press2007} computed by re-fitting the parabolic minimum
on each of the $N{-}1$ subsamples that drop one deep sensor at a time.
The retrieval has exactly one free parameter; per-site contrasts use
the pooled jackknife variance of the two sites.

\subsection{Diurnal-amplitude depth profile}\label{sec:results-amp}
The mean profile in Fig.~\ref{fig:meanT} only constrains the
\emph{DC level} of the subsurface temperature; the amplitude of the
diurnal wave tests the model's frequency-domain response. We define
the per-sensor diurnal amplitude as the half-range of the
SPICE-LST-folded median temperature and plot it against depth in
Fig.~\ref{fig:amplitude}. Above $z\!=\!\zborestem$ the observed
amplitude rises sharply above the modelled regolith attenuation curve
--- the borestem signature (\S\ref{sec:borestem}). Below
$\zborestem$ both models predict an amplitude
$\lesssim\!10^{-7}\,\si{\kelvin}$ ($\sim\!18$ e-foldings of the
diurnal skin depth $\delta\sim$3--5\,cm, eq.~\ref{eq:K}--\ref{eq:cp}),
which is two to three orders of magnitude below the digitisation noise
floor of the restored 1971--1977 record ($\sim\!0.05$--$0.2\,
\si{\kelvin}$). The deep-sensor amplitudes therefore carry no
useful information on $\kappa = K/(\rho c_p)$ at the in-situ scale;
the diurnal panel functions exclusively as a borestem diagnostic,
not as a frequency-domain validation. Per-sensor phase-folded
diurnal cycles for every Apollo HFE sensor (deep \emph{and} shallow)
are reproduced as Figs.~S1--S2.

\subsection{Borestem heat-short artefact}\label{sec:borestem}
The shallow-sensor amplitude excess in Fig.~\ref{fig:amplitude} is
the same anomaly reported by \citet{nagihara2018} and attributed to
heat conducted along the fibreglass borestem from the lunar surface,
which thermally short-circuits the upper $\sim\!\zborestem$ of
regolith. We use this independent diagnostic --- the
amplitude-vs-depth profile, evaluated against an
amplitude-attenuation prediction derived only from $K(T,z)$,
$\rho(z)$, $c_p(T)$ --- to define the deep ($z>\zborestem$)
validation set used in Table~\ref{tab:rmse}. The exclusion is
therefore not based on the residuals it produces.

\subsection{Robustness to parameter uncertainties}\label{sec:robustness}
Fig.~\ref{fig:sensitivity} sweeps the three least-constrained
\emph{auxiliary} ingredients of the \Hayne\ model: the basal heat flux
$Q_b$ (factor-2 range bracketing the
\citealt{langseth1976}/\citealt{saito2007,nagihara2018} debate),
the radiative coefficient $\chi$ (\Hayne\ default 2.7 vs
\Vas\ 0.0--3.0), and the specific-heat parameterisation
(Hemingway--Robie--Wilson polynomial vs \citealt{biele2022} rational
fit). Across the entire published-uncertainty envelope the deep RMSE
shifts by $<\SI{0.5}{\kelvin}$ at both sites; \S\ref{sec:results-kd}
treats $K_d$ separately as the controlled retrieval parameter.

\subsection{Per-site $K_d$ retrieval}\label{sec:results-kd}
Fig.~\ref{fig:kdsweep} shows the deep-sensor RMSE
(eq.~\ref{eq:rmse-kd}) as a function of $K_d$ at each landing site,
with the \Hayne\ functional form (eqs.~\ref{eq:K}--\ref{eq:cp})
otherwise unchanged. Both curves have a clean, well-resolved minimum
within the swept range. The parabolic point estimates and
leave-one-out jackknife uncertainties are
\[
K_{d,\mathrm{A15}}^{*}=4.85\pm 0.59\,\si{\milli\watt\per\meter\per\kelvin},\qquad
K_{d,\mathrm{A17}}^{*}=11.23\pm 0.61\,\si{\milli\watt\per\meter\per\kelvin},
\]
with corresponding deep-sensor RMSE\,$=0.90$\,K ($N=7$) and $0.30$\,K
($N=16$) respectively. The retrieval improves on the published-$K_d$
\Hayne\ baseline at both sites (Table~\ref{tab:rmse}: $1.38\to 0.90$
at A15, $2.68\to 0.30$ at A17), and at A17 it is also a small but
non-trivial improvement on the M\&S 3-layer fit ($0.65\to 0.30$ K).
The bias collapses to $\le 0.03$\,K at both sites, confirming that
$K_d$ is the principal lever between the \Hayne\ form and the in-situ
HFE record once the borestem zone is excluded.

The two-site contrast is
$\Delta K_d^{*} = 6.4\pm 0.9\,\si{\milli\watt\per\meter\per\kelvin}$
on the pooled jackknife variance, which corresponds to a normalised
two-site difference of $\Delta K_d^{*} /
\sqrt{\sigma_{K_d,\mathrm{A15}}^{2}+\sigma_{K_d,\mathrm{A17}}^{2}}
\simeq 7.5$. The two retrievals therefore disagree at high
significance, and \emph{both} reject the global value
$K_d=3.4\,\si{\milli\watt\per\meter\per\kelvin}$ adopted by
\Hayne\ at $\geq 2\sigma$ ($2.5\sigma$ at A15, $12.9\sigma$ at A17).
We discuss the unavoidable $K_d/Q_b$ degeneracy of single-borehole
inversions --- which makes the absolute A17 number, but not the
per-site contrast, model-dependent --- in \S\ref{sec:disc-kd}.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}\label{sec:disc}

\paragraph{Per-site $K_d$ disagreement is the headline result.}\label{sec:disc-kd}
The central scientific finding (Fig.~\ref{fig:kdsweep},
\S\ref{sec:results-kd}) is that the deep conductivity required by
the in-situ Apollo HFE record is not the same at the two landing
sites under the \Hayne\ functional form: A15 demands
$K_d^{*}=4.85\pm 0.59$ and A17 demands $K_d^{*}=11.23\pm 0.61\,
\si{\milli\watt\per\meter\per\kelvin}$, a contrast of
$\sim\!7.5\sigma$ on the leave-one-out jackknife. Both values reject
the global \Hayne\ value $3.4\,\si{\milli\watt\per\meter\per\kelvin}$
at $\geq 2\sigma$. Because the only thing that differs between the
two retrievals is the latitude, albedo, basal flux, and the in-situ
deep temperatures themselves, the disagreement cannot be absorbed
into the \Hayne\ shape, the radiative multiplier, the heat capacity
parameterisation, or the surface forcing chain (all of which are
identical at A15 and A17 in our pipeline). It is a real
\emph{regional} contrast in the conductivity that the regolith
delivers to a buried thermometer.

\paragraph{The $K_d/Q_b$ degeneracy.}
Single-borehole inversions of buried thermometers cannot break the
degeneracy between deep conductivity and basal heat flux at steady
state: the deep gradient is $|\partial_z T|=Q_b/K(T_d)$, so any
multiplicative rescaling $(K_d,Q_b)\to(\alpha K_d,\alpha Q_b)$
leaves the equilibrium profile invariant in the conduction-dominated
limit. The absolute A17 retrieval is therefore tied to our adopted
$Q_{b,\mathrm{A17}}=15\,\si{\milli\watt\per\meter\squared}$
(\citealt{langseth1976,nagihara2018}). If the
\citet{saito2007,nagihara2018} reanalyses imply $Q_{b,\mathrm{A17}}$
closer to $\sim\!10\,\si{\milli\watt\per\meter\squared}$, the
degeneracy rescales the A17 retrieval to
$K_d^{*}\sim 7.5\,\si{\milli\watt\per\meter\per\kelvin}$, comparable
to the M\&S 3-layer deep value. Crucially, the per-site
\emph{contrast} is invariant under any single global rescaling of
$Q_b$ because it differences out, so the
\textbf{$\Delta K_d^{*}\!\sim\!6.4\,\si{\milli\watt\per\meter\per\kelvin}$
result is the robust statement}; the absolute A17 value should be
quoted with the explicit caveat that it is conditioned on the chosen
$Q_b$.

\paragraph{Why the M\&S 3-layer profile recovered A17 so well.}
The M\&S 3-layer parameters were tuned against the same HFE deep
gradient, so the column-2 success at A17 in Table~\ref{tab:rmse} is
a fit residual rather than independent confirmation. Our $K_d^{*}$
retrieval (column 3) recovers a deep conductivity within $\sim\!2\times$
of the M\&S deep layer ($11.2$ vs.\ $6.3\,\si{\milli\watt\per\meter\per\kelvin}$),
which --- together with the $K_d/Q_b$ caveat above --- frames the
M\&S deep layer as a particular point in the
$(K_d,Q_b,\mathrm{shape})$ family of solutions consistent with the
restored HFE record, not as a uniquely preferred parameterisation.

\paragraph{The borestem identification is independent of residuals.}
The shallow-sensor exclusion (\S\ref{sec:borestem}) is read directly
off Fig.~\ref{fig:amplitude} --- the observed amplitude departs from
the modelled attenuation curve at $z\!\sim\!\zborestem$ at both sites
\emph{independently}. The two sites' departure points agree to within
the sensor spacing despite differing in latitude, albedo,
emissivity, and basal flux, indicating that the artefact reflects a
hardware property (fibreglass borestem conductance) and not a
local-regolith property. This is the strongest argument for the
$z<\zborestem$ exclusion that we know how to make from the data
alone, and it precedes the deep-RMSE statistics in
Table~\ref{tab:rmse} and the $K_d$ retrieval in
Fig.~\ref{fig:kdsweep}.

\paragraph{Robustness of the auxiliary parameters.}
Across the full $Q_b$ debate, the \Hayne / \Vas\ disagreement on
$\chi$, and the choice of $c_p(T)$ parameterisation, the deep RMSE
moves by $<\SI{0.5}{\kelvin}$ at both sites
(Fig.~\ref{fig:sensitivity}). The corresponding shift in the
retrieved $K_d^{*}$ is small compared to the per-site contrast:
$Q_b$ rescales the absolute value (per the degeneracy above) but
leaves the contrast invariant, $\chi$ enters $K(T,z)$ only through
the radiative multiplier and produces $\lesssim\!10\%$ shifts in the
parabolic minimum, and $c_p$ enters only through the spin-up rate
(not the steady-state mean). The
$\sim\!7.5\sigma$ per-site contrast is therefore not an artefact of
any single under-constrained \Hayne\ ingredient.

\paragraph{Software-engineering closure.}
Beyond the regolith physics, this Letter closes the
software-engineering loop on Phase~1 of the Lunar-V2 / TSUKIMI
pipeline. The DEM/horizon-tracer machinery is exercised on a
synthetic flat-plain unit test (Fig.~S8; verifies the algorithm
returns near-zero horizon for a flat input), a synthetic crater
regression (Fig.~S9), and an end-to-end shadow-coupled solver
closure to $<\SI{0.05}{\kelvin}$ (Fig.~S11). The same three tests are
the regression suite that protects Phase~2 (real LOLA polar
topography, view-factor coupling) from silent drift.

\section{Conclusion}
A self-consistent SPICE-driven 1-D Crank--Nicolson solver applied to
the restored Apollo HFE record yields a single-parameter retrieval of
the deep conductivity $K_d$ under the \Hayne\ functional form. The
two landing sites disagree at high significance:
$K_{d,\mathrm{A15}}^{*}=4.85\pm 0.59$ and
$K_{d,\mathrm{A17}}^{*}=11.23\pm 0.61\,\si{\milli\watt\per\meter\per\kelvin}$,
a per-site contrast of $\sim\!7.5\sigma$ on the leave-one-out
jackknife, and both retrievals reject the global \Hayne\ value
$3.4\,\si{\milli\watt\per\meter\per\kelvin}$ at $\geq 2\sigma$. The
contrast is invariant under the $K_d/Q_b$ degeneracy that conditions
the absolute A17 number, which is therefore the robust claim. The
borestem heat-short artefact above $z\!=\!\zborestem$ is identified
\emph{a priori} from the diurnal-amplitude depth profile rather than
from residuals, yielding a hardware-based exclusion criterion that is
identical at A15 and A17 and isolates the deep-sensor RMSE used in
the retrieval. Phase~1 of the Lunar-V2 / TSUKIMI pipeline is thereby
validated end-to-end, with the same SPICE-driven solver and a-priori
borestem cut now ready to be exercised on real LOLA polar topography
in Phase~2.

\section*{Limitations and journal fit}
With five main-text figures (Figs.~\ref{fig:hayne-props}--\ref{fig:kdsweep}),
a quantitative single-parameter retrieval, and a book-like SI
(Figs.~S1--S11), the natural venue for this submission is
JGR:Planets rather than a GRL Letter. The single highest-priority
follow-up that we have deliberately deferred is a real-LOLA horizon
test at the A15/A17 sites to replace the synthetic-flat-plain unit
test (Fig.~S8); this is a closure test for the Phase-2 polar
pipeline rather than a regolith-physics result and naturally belongs
to the Phase-2 paper. The dataset and code archived under the
\texttt{Open Research} statement should be Zenodo-DOI'd (not
branch-named) before the AGU submission.

Three caveats that the reader should keep in mind when interpreting
the per-site $K_d$ retrieval: \emph{(i)} the absolute values are
conditional on the adopted $Q_b$ (\S\ref{sec:disc-kd}); the per-site
contrast is invariant under any global $Q_b$ rescaling but the
absolute A17 number is not; \emph{(ii)} the retrieval assumes the
\Hayne\ functional form for $K(T,z)$ is correct and varies only
$K_d$ --- a discrete-layer or piecewise-linear shape (e.g.\
\citealt{martinez2021}) is a separate hypothesis class that we do
not test in this Letter; and \emph{(iii)} the diurnal-amplitude panel
(Fig.~\ref{fig:amplitude}) is used \emph{only} as a borestem
diagnostic, not as a frequency-domain validation, because below
$\zborestem$ both models predict amplitudes well below the
1971--1977 digitisation noise floor.
